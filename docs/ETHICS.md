# Responsible use

This project studies the language of tweets about suicide. Such words come from real people at very hard moments. Please read this page before using the code or the results.

## What this code is

* A **research comparison** of text-classification methods on one small public dataset.
* A teaching example of careful evaluation: baselines, confidence intervals, significance tests and cross-validation.

## What this code is not

* **Not a diagnostic or screening tool.** A model's output says something about a piece of text, not about a person's mental health.
* **Not ready for deployment.** The best model still misses about 1 in 13 suicidal tweets and wrongly flags about 1 in 6 ordinary ones on our test set. Real-world error rates would likely be worse.
* **Not a substitute for human care.**

## If you build on this work

* Keep a **trained human in the loop** for any decision that affects a real person.
* Use alerts to **offer support** (for example, helpline information), never to punish, expose or embarrass anyone.
* Follow privacy law and platform terms; do not collect or publish identifiable data.
* Work with **mental health professionals** on labelling, thresholds and what happens after an alert.
* Remember that false alarms can stigmatise people, and missed cases can give false comfort.

## Data handling in this project

* Only a public dataset licensed for non-commercial research (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) is used.
* The tweets are not redistributed in this repository.
* Usernames and links are removed during cleaning.
* Examples shown in the paper are made up, not quoted from real users.

## Need help?

If you or someone you know is struggling, please reach out to a local crisis helpline, a trusted person, or a mental health professional.
